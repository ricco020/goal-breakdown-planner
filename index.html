Goal breakdown planner: milestones, weekly pace, and where you stand today

Works for any goal you can put a number on, going up (savings, pages written, clients) or going down (debt left, weight, minutes per mile). Give the starting number, the target and the finish date. You get a dated milestone at the end of every month, the amount to move each week, and, once you enter where you are now, whether you are ahead or behind the line and the new weekly pace that still gets you there on time.

If-then plans (what is likely to get in the way, and what you will do then)

How the milestones are dated

The plan draws a straight line from the starting number on the start date to the target on the finish date, counted in days. Each milestone is the value of that line on the last day of a calendar month (a 31-day month moves the line a little more than February), and the last one is the target on the finish date. The weekly pace is the total change divided by the number of weeks. Nothing is rounded until it is displayed, so the milestones add up exactly.

Ahead or behind, and the re-planned pace

"Where you are now" is compared to the line at today's date. Ahead means you have moved further than the line in the direction of the goal (higher for a goal that goes up, lower for one that goes down). The required weekly pace is what is left divided by the weeks left: when you are behind it is higher than the original pace, which tells you early whether the finish date still holds or should move. The next 12 weekly checkpoints are dated from today on that new pace, not from the original plan, because a plan that still asks for last month's numbers is the one people abandon.

Limits

A straight line assumes steady progress. Goals that start slow (running, a new habit) or that grow on themselves (investments with returns) will sit behind the line early and catch up later: read the gap as information, not as a verdict. Nothing is sent anywhere: the plan is stored in this browser only.
